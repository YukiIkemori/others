Background art for a 2D command RPG: ONE complete, seamless field map seen in the classic JRPG overhead 3/4 view (orthographic, camera tilted about 50 degrees down, looking north; no perspective convergence, vertical walls show their south faces). The place: "The Abbey Where the Bell Never Rings" - a ruined monastery on a lonely cliff-top high above a sea of clouds, at dusk, in a world with two moons.

STYLE: premium high-resolution PIXEL ART, drawn by the same pixel artist who draws the game's character sprites: crisp square pixels, deliberate pixel clusters, dark coloured outlines around every object (deep plum / indigo, never pure black), no blur, no soft airbrush, no painterly smearing, no photographic texture. The richest hand-pixelled backgrounds of 32-bit era JRPGs combined with a modern HD-2D sense of light. Every stone block, crack, moss patch, grass tuft, root and leaf is described. Hue-shifted shading: shadows turn blue-violet and saturated, lights turn warm peach-gold. Deep, layered values and overlapping forms: this must feel like a place with real depth and height, never a flat tile map.

LIGHT: the last afterglow of dusk comes low from the upper left: every raised thing casts a long shadow to the lower right, wall tops and left faces catch peach-gold light, south faces sit in cool violet shade. Hanging iron lanterns with amber glass burn at the gate, along the paths, at the nave doorway, on the stairs and at the tower door; each throws a warm pool of orange light on the ground and nearby stone. Overall key: rich, moody twilight, but NOT murky: paths, stairs and floors are clearly readable pale warm stone; saturated accents (crimson ivy and maple leaves, gold and ruby stained glass, teal moss, pale blue moonlit stone, small white and violet wild flowers).

LAYOUT (portrait; top = north = far; the ground rises in three terraces toward the top; every terrace edge is a visible stone retaining wall showing its south face, so height reads clearly). The abbey stands on an island of rock: beyond its outer walls and cliffs, at the top and down both sides, there is only open sky and the sea of clouds far below.

The tower spire, broken gables and cypress tops cut against this sky.

GAMEPLAY READABILITY: every walkable surface (flagstone paths, plazas, stairs, the nave floor) is clearly paler, flatter and cleaner than what cannot be walked on (grass with graves, shrubs, walls, water, cliff). All walkable areas connect into one continuous route from the bottom steps to the tower door and the overlook. Paths and stairs are generous: about one eighth of the image width. Keep paths free of obstacles. Scale: a standing person would be about 190 px tall; doorways about 280 px tall; headstones about 90 px.

Architecture is ornate and organic, never boxy: buttresses, pinnacles, tracery, moulded arch orders, carved capitals, statues in niches, weathered and chipped edges, leaning stones, sagging lines, rubble, roots lifting the paving.
